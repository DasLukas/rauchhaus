# Architecture

## Runtime and boundaries

The application is a static, German-language TypeScript client. Vite builds the entry point, CSS, local font and Three.js renderer. The house selector links to the same page with a house query parameter. The small house is the default. Only the selected GLB is requested, from the same origin. Each page load creates its own camera, markers and simulation. The application has no account system, API, analytics or persistent student records. Reloading starts a clean exploration. Only the repository link in the adult help section navigates off site, after deliberate activation.

## Modules and data flow

| Module | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `src/simulation` | House topology, source locations, detector elevations, deterministic smoke transport and optional compartment fire development. Independent of the DOM, Three.js and frame rate. |
| `src/ui` | German learning activities, settings, native dialog, marker buttons, icons and experiment state. |
| `src/scene` | Orthographic camera, physical-object hit testing, indicator projection, lighting, smoke volumes and visible flames. |
| `src/main.ts` | Connects controls and time updates to the active simulation and presents status changes. |
| `assets/blender` and `public/models` | Source artwork, repeatable generator and batched web export. |

A source selection starts a fresh run with the current door settings. A door change updates connectivity while retaining the existing upper layers. The animation clock advances the active simulation and passes an immutable snapshot to the renderer. The renderer projects interaction anchors to viewport coordinates. The DOM uses those positions for keyboard- and touch-accessible controls. Pause, an open help dialog and a backgrounded page suspend the teaching clock. A guided activity owns a second simulation instance and restores the original experiment on exit. Both trial arms share the same source, initial conditions and observation time, apart from the living-room door setting.

Both house sizes share the simulation rules, coefficients, settings and learning activities. Layout data defines the geometry, room connections, fixtures and controls. Geometry can affect when an observation occurs. Changes to smoke and fire behaviour should be verified in both layouts. Free fire tests exercise every selectable source with the default fire setup and with its room doors deliberately closed.

## Smoke model

Each room and landing tracks the volume, tracer mass and warmth of an upper layer above a lower air zone. Both houses use the same sustained source profile when fire is off. The plume develops at the initial rate for the first seven model seconds, then the delivery rate increases linearly to twice its initial peak by second 16 and holds there. Emissions reach the layer after the rise from the source to the local ceiling. As a source room fills, the remaining air limits further layer growth. The gas already in a full source room gradually approaches the concentration and warmth of the source mixture. The sloped roof changes both the rise distance and the volume above a given layer height.

The first arriving plume sets the origin of a ceiling current. Its footprint expands with warmth and available gas volume. A doorway starts to carry upper-layer gas when the current reaches it and the layer extends below the lintel. Flow grows with doorway width and submerged depth and is reduced by opposing flow from the receiving room. Closed doors permit small leakage. Each landing must receive the current before it can carry smoke farther up the stairwell. The simulation tracks entrainment and cooling. Each fixed 1/30-s step computes transfers from the same starting state. Competing openings share the available donor volume and the receiver's free capacity. Only accepted gas carries tracer and heat out of a donor. The cumulative tracer generated by the source equals the inventory in the house plus the tracer lost through the exterior entrance.

Closing a door can slow the spread while residual smoke remains visible. The cutaway front acts as a closed boundary. Wind, detailed flame chemistry and toxic exposure are outside the model. Its time scale and transport coefficients serve repeatable qualitative comparisons. The app uses no CFAST runtime or calibrated building prediction.

At a stair opening, the balance of upward and downward transport depends on the layers on the two adjoining landings. As the upper layer deepens toward the stair opening, less clear air remains above the rising current. A deep layer can then feed smoke down into the headroom of the next landing. The same rule applies at each opening, including the basement stairs in the large house. Changing a door can therefore change when the lower landings receive smoke.

An incoming current entrains less clear air as its receiving compartment fills. Where smoke layers meet across an open connection, they can exchange equal volumes of gas even when their net volume flow is zero. This transfers tracer and heat between the layers while preserving their volumes. A sustained source can therefore enrich a full staircase, and the renderer continues to derive its grey tones from the local tracer concentration.

Detectors respond when the ceiling current reaches their position, the layer descends to their sensor height and the concentration exceeds their threshold. A quiet symbol and accessible announcement show activation.

The renderer reads each ceiling current's origin, reach and remaining rise distance from the snapshot. Each open doorway also reports how long gas has flowed in its current direction. Opening a previously closed door starts a new plume at the lintel even when some smoke has already leaked into the receiving room. The plume widens as room air mixes into its edges and merges with the upper layer. In the stairwell, rising smoke widens on its way to the next landing. A downward current first appears just beneath the stair opening. Its reach grows with the duration of actual downward flow and follows the lower edge of the accumulating layer. Both the free experiment and jumps to a guided observation time use the same simulation clock.

The shader clips smoke to the rooms, openings and roof and depth-tests each slice against furniture. Exponential attenuation accumulates along the view path. Local smoke concentration controls the grey tone, while a shared noise texture animates the interface and variations within dense smoke.

## Optional compartment fire

`SmokeSimulation.setFireEnabled` enables or clears a fire in the selected source compartment. `LearningSession` prepares the same free fire experiment in either layout. It keeps existing open connections and supplies one open door for each selectable source room that would otherwise start closed. Changing the fire setting restarts the session and resets the camera, just like the restart button. Both actions restore the current door preset. Selecting a source keeps all door positions. The door-comparison lesson uses its own smoke-only instance.

The fire model advances on the same fixed clock as gas transport. Burning consumes the room’s available air, and open connections supply air according to the adjoining compartment. Local growth and secondary burning determine plume delivery, and air depletion increases the soot yield. The depth and warmth of the gas layer influence gas flaming and radiant heating. Exposed contents accumulate heat over time. Sufficient heating and air availability allow rapid secondary burning. These quantities use normalized classroom units. The model has no material-specific ignition predictions.

The immutable snapshot exposes local flame strength, flaming in the upper layer and secondary involvement in the source room. The guided fire activity stops when gas flames and then secondary flames reach visible intensities. Skipping ahead advances the same model to the observation. Ending the activity restores the free experiment and its pause state.

The active compartment renders flame emission on the same depth-tested slices as the smoke. A deterministic sampler uses the selected source and model clock to place brief pockets of burning gas. Each pocket appears as a thin, gently curling tongue that tapers at both ends along the local smoke boundary. As the boundary descends into the lower half of the room, the demonstration fades out these tongues. The first lesson stop samples the same burst envelope to hold a visible moment. Separate patches place secondary flames on exposed furnishings, textiles and foliage in the batched mesh. Each room’s fire shader uses its own patch count. When rearranging furniture, update the floor-relative coordinates in `src/scene/fuels.ts`. The model check verifies their room boundaries, and the browser test checks visible surface ignition at the plant and sofa in the small house. Reduced motion holds each tongue’s shape and position steady during its fade in and out.

## Asset contract and rendering

The web asset uses glTF coordinates, with positive Y up, X to the right and positive Z toward the viewer. Ground-floor elevation is 0. The storeys are spaced 2.4 model units apart. A right-hand staircase connects four landings in the small house. A central staircase serves the five levels of the large house, including the basement. Each apartment has its own entrance on the landing. Each source, detector and door has one named `anchor_<id>` node. A `hinge_<doorId>` node owns the moving door mesh and its opening-angle metadata. The application positions controls from those nodes. The selected floor plan in `src/simulation/layout-small.json` or `src/simulation/layout.json` supplies the room boundaries, floor elevations, openings and interaction positions to the Blender generator and the teaching model. Doors sit flush in straight partitions and swing a quarter turn into the receiving room. Furniture placement reserves the full swept area of each door leaf and handle. The small house has an interactive entrance through the right wall. Its leaf opens outward onto a side landing.

Detector anchors locate the indicator lenses on the modeled housings. The simulation tests the local smoke layer height, density and horizontal coverage at each sensor. Active lenses share a pulse derived from simulation time. The UI uses the same pulse for a single Web Audio tone, enabled by a user gesture. Pausing holds active lights steady and silences the tone. Resetting the house clears the session and restores the camera while retaining the sound preference.

The GLB batches static geometry and paints vertex colors, including baked contact shading. Separate pivots keep door animation cheap. The renderer limits resolution and reduces it during sustained slow animation. Unchanged paused or empty scenes avoid repeated drawing. The smoke renderer shares a noise texture across rooms and skips empty compartments.

The overview fits the selected house from its lowest storey to the ridge. Tapping a storey focuses its controls and allows panning. The DOM retains each marker's projected anchor and spaces neighbouring targets across the same storey so the two entrance doors stay individually tappable. A short line connects displaced targets to their anchors.

## Build and deployment

Only the checked static artifact is deployed. Pull requests have read access to repository content. Uploading the website and requesting the GitHub Pages OIDC token are restricted to the publishing path on `main`. The deploy job references the Pages environment. Reverting a change on `main` republishes the previous source through the same checks. There is no database migration or server-side application state.
